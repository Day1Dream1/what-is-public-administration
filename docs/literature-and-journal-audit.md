## Publisher spot checks

The [publisher sample](../pipeline/audit/publisher-sample.csv) records 75 items from selected volumes and issues across all five journals. Each identifier was found in the locally downloaded OpenAlex candidates. The audit makes 32 keep decisions, 40 drop decisions, and leaves 3 documented cases open. The selections target likely edge cases and useful controls. They do not estimate a corpus-wide error rate or establish exhaustive coverage of a volume. Verify by matching DOI or OpenAlex ID. A blank decision records a publisher section without adjudicating eligibility.

### Historical PAR pagination

The historical metadata frequently repeat the first page as the last page. In selected years the downloaded OpenAlex `article` or `review` records had identical first and last fields for 103 of 104 candidates in 1980, 128 of 128 in 1985, 103 of 103 in 1990, 85 of 85 in 1995, and 57 of 58 in 1999. This pattern is a source limitation. The JSTOR journal archive identifies “Dynamics in Rural Policy Development” at pages 24–28 and a review of flexible work hours at pages 72–85; both records had only their first page in OpenAlex. A 1999 article by Folz covers pages 336–345 and both API sources returned only page 336. The 11-page Hartle example is explicitly a book review despite its OpenAlex type. Treat scalar historical JSTOR pagination as unknown evidence for length; use the archived section and complete page ranges when available. [1980 TOC](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i239991) · [1990 TOC](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i240044) · [Folz's published article](https://drexel.edu/greatworks/Theme/Fall/~/media/Files/greatworks/pdf_FL10/WK8_4_Holz_1999.ashx) · [Hartle's book review](https://www.jstor.org/stable/976156)

### PAR practitioner material

The audit applies a research-corpus rule. Keep research articles and substantive scholarly syntheses, including work written to inform practitioners. Drop confirmed editorials, interviews, lectures, commissioned Viewpoints, and introductory notes. Do not infer a publisher section from a topic, page count, or phrase in a title alone. For example, the same issue contains established research articles and full-length Viewpoints. The publisher's older Theory to Practice guidance also welcomed research reviews. [Wiley 83(3) TOC](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/15406210/2023/83/3) · [Theory to Practice contributor guidance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/puar.12658)

- **Drop** the one-page Theory to Practice introduction, DOI `10.1111/j.1540-6210.2009.02108_1.x`. The next entry in the section is a separate article. [Wiley 70(1) TOC](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/15406210/2010/70/1)
- **Keep** Engbers and Rubin (2018), DOI `10.1111/puar.12925`. The published first page reproduced by vLex labels it a Research Article and notes that it was originally accepted for Theory to Practice. The Wiley page was inaccessible during this check. [Published article reproduction](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/theory-to-practice-policy-900908967)
- **Leave open** the 2008 Administrative Profile and environmental modeling essay. The excerpts and section names establish their identity. Further content review would be needed for a categorical decision. The 1988 Governance Country Report is also deferred. [PAR 68(3) TOC](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/15406210/2008/68/3) · [Governance 1(1) TOC](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/14680491/1988/1/1)

Some publisher endpoints returned access errors. Individual Wiley article records were used for the 2021 PAR examples when the full issue page was inaccessible. Taylor & Francis TOC evidence was available via its public issue listings indexed by search, even where opening the bare URL directly returned an access error. The audit applies only to cited records and leaves metadata-only section assignments tentative outside the sample.

## Verified literature for interpreting the map

- **Peters, B. G., Pierre, J., Sørensen, E., and Torfing, J. (2022).** “Bringing political science back into public administration research.” *Governance*, 35(4), 962–982. Table 1 lists the exact five journals used here. The authors searched Web of Science abstracts from 2005–2019 for politics, power, and democracy, a useful precedent for journal selection. Their keyword counts and denominators come from a different index and sampling method. [Article and Table 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/gove.12705)
- **Walker, R. M., Zhang, J., and Chandra, Y. (2024).** *Topics in Public Administration*. Cambridge University Press. A topic analysis of 12,760 articles from 17 journals for 1991–2019 identifies 50 topics. Its broader journal frame offers an external point of comparison for themes in this map. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/abs/topics-in-public-administration/C9A9E7C04AF1DEAACB4EC7BBB9423DB5)
- **Vogel, R., and Hattke, F. (2022).** “A century of Public Administration: Traveling through time and topics.” *Public Administration*, 100(1), 17–40. The study retrieves the journal's 1923–2020 record, combines bibliometrics with natural language processing, and follows 63 thematic clusters across five eras. It is a comparison for PA's historical evolution and for reporting uneven source coverage. [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/padm.12831)
- **Ni, C., Sugimoto, C. R., and Robbin, A. (2017).** “Examining the Evolution of the Field of Public Administration through a Bibliometric Analysis of Public Administration Review.” *Public Administration Review*, 77(4), 496–509. The authors analyze the journal's first 75 years. Their historical perspective helps interpret changes in PAR's publishing mix. [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/puar.12737)
- **Osborne, S. P. (2017).** “Public management research over the decades: what are we writing about?” *Public Management Review*, 19(2), 109–113. A review of 211 papers across two periods, 1999–2002 and 2015–2016, with explicit documentation of PMR's early title. [Article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14719037.2016.1252142)
- **Rajan et al. (2024).** “Changing discourse of public administration: Where PAD stands?” *Public Administration and Development*, 44(4), 280–297. A dynamic topic model on research articles from nine journals over 2010–2022 gives a comparison for interpreting multi-journal shares and changing themes. [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/pad.2047)
- **Kim, K., Kim, M. H., and Peters, B. G. (2026).** “Tracing the Trends of Governance in Governance From 1988 to 2023: Achievements and Future Prospects.” *Governance*, 39(2), e70125. The abstract dates the BERTopic and qualitative analysis to 1990–2023. The title also describes the journal's longer history. [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/gove.70125)
- **LePere-Schloop, M., and Nesbit, R. (2023).** “The nexus of public administration, public policy, and nonprofit studies: An empirical mapping of research topics and knowledge integration.” *Public Administration Review*, 83(3), 486–502. Citation analysis and qualitative coding offer a comparison for interpreting overlaps across adjacent fields and a precedent for future citation analysis. [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/puar.13587)
